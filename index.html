About

A little about me.

I am from Chandler, Arizona, and I have chased the same question for as long as I can remember. What makes the brain work, and can a machine be taught to do the same? That thread runs through everything I do, from three ASU research labs to the coding bootcamps I founded at the Chandler Sunset Library.

I am a current Computer Science student at Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, and I am exploring problems at the intersection of Neuroscience and Computer Science.

About

A little about me.

I am always asking how machines can be trained to think like humans. Specifically, I want to bridge the gap between biological intelligence and artificial neural networks by optimizing how deep learning architectures retain complex data.

I focus my work on the intersection of computational neuroscience and computer science. I have built machine learning pipelines to analyze colon cancer patient data. I have also utilized mathematical equations to build and model neural networks. Along with this, I have used AlphaFold to visualize protein folding. Driven by a commitment to expand AI and computer science education in my community, I also work with the Mark Cuban AI Foundation. Through my work, I have reached over 1000 students and educators nationwide.

Projects

Selected projects.

Nest

↗

A mobile roommate-management app: one shared home for chores, bills, groceries, and communication. Its standout feature turns a grocery run into fair, item-by-item payback. The buyer scans the receipt, Nest pulls out each item and price, everyone claims what they used, and it calculates exactly what each person owes. My team and I built Nest, and it won 1st place in the "10 Minutes Back" track at Cal Poly CodeBox Hacks.

Predicting Colorectal Cancer Treatments

↗

A machine learning pipeline trained on 1,107 real de-identified patients from the GENIE BPC CRC dataset to predict survival, recommend chemotherapy, and flag bacterial therapy candidates. Random Forest with 5 fold cross validation reached AUC 0.68 against a 0.50 baseline. Presented at AzSEF 2026 and DataPaths.
